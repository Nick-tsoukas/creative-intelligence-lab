# MA-005 — Relative color and repeated frame: Josef Albers, *Study for Homage to the Square: Light Rising*

- **Creator / date / medium:** Josef Albers, **1950, altered 1959**, oil on wood fiberboard, 81.3 × 81.3 cm. NGA collection 1992.28.1.
- **Institutional source:** https://www.nga.gov/artworks/74822-study-homage-square-light-rising
- **Representation inspected:** **cropped NGA-origin image preview** on 2026-11-15, not the full square in reliable color; full structure is separately documented by NGA's text/visual description. The snippet displays a grayish field and high-contrast yellow region. **Do not claim to have seen all square relationships pixel-for-pixel.**
- **Rights:** museum explicitly notes the object's media is not downloadable and advises requesting image-use permission. **No image is copied into the Lab.**

## What can and cannot be observed
From the accessible partial image, a broad neutral field and luminous yellow square create a strong local interaction. The **institution** describes the entire composition as nested light-blue outer, gray intermediary and yellow inner squares, offset in a characteristic placement. Those specific full-frame relations are institution-reported, not fully visually inspected by this study.

## What the institution says
The NGA title is *Study for Homage to the Square: Light Rising* (1950, altered 1959). Surface, support, pigment details and reworking on the back are documented on the original catalog page. The work belongs to a series that studies relationships among color areas; describing it as simply a yellow button on a gray background misses the artistic investigation.

## Interpretation / limits
**Hypothesis:** perceived color tone, intensity and hierarchy depend on adjacency and area, not only a component's hex value. **Alternative:** the influence of colors in this original painting cannot yield actual WCAG text contrast compliance or product-specific emotional response without tests.

## Transferable mechanism
**Use when:** a product has dark/light image surfaces, call-to-action accents and changing backgrounds, or artists' photographs create unpredictable color adjacency.

**Don't use when:** a designer cites artistic contrast as an exemption to accessible contrast ratios, focus visibility, text color tokens or clear semantic warning states.

## Original UI exercise
Hold the **same** CTA, type, token palette and labels constant. Create 3 surrounding surface/photography contexts; compare perceived salience and remeasure actual contrast and focus. Record image-source truth and responsive changes. Conclude only observed differences, not "Albers predicts conversion."
