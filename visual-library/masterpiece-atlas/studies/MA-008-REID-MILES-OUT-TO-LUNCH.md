# MA-008 — Music identity as a visual instrument: Reid Miles, *Out to Lunch!* album cover

- **Creator / date / format:** Cover design by Reid Miles, Blue Note Records BLP 4163, Eric Dolphy *Out to Lunch!*, originally released in 1964. The cover photograph is **attributed to Reid Miles in a specialist discographic source**; Blue Note confirms his album-cover design but does not separately specify this photograph's creator on the page examined.
- **Record-label primary documentation:** Blue Note's own 2019 original retrospective, naming the cover in the Reid Miles poster edition: https://www.bluenote.com/blue-note-wall-art/ ; original album commentary https://www.bluenote.com/spotlight/out-to-lunch-with-eric-dolphy/ .
- **Visual representation directly inspected (2026-11-15):** a photographed record sleeve/cover preview with apparent aging and color effects reproduced by independent music collector (NOT a verified color-calibrated original press): https://londonjazzcollector.wordpress.com/2013/04/12/the-six-worst-and-six-best-blue-note-covers/ . Blue Note original image rights are separate.
- **Rights:** album sleeve design/photography protected; do not reproduce the original cover in a product or distribute image files without permission.

## OBSERVED album-cover preview
A bold black all-caps title occupies a horizontal white strip in the upper area, below a much slimmer line of musician credits and above an unusual shop-door photo tinted blue. The photograph contains a “WILL BE BACK” sign and a clock-like illustration with several directions/arms. Cropped wall/door edges divide the lower area into irregular vertical blocks. The performer name above is comparatively narrow and airy, providing a controlled release from the lower title's black weight.

## PRIMARY LABEL CONTEXT
Blue Note explicitly ties its visual identity to **Reid Miles's graphic design**, photography, instrument recording and musician culture. Its 2019 release lists *Out to Lunch!* among commemorated classic covers. The label's album narrative characterizes the music as unexpected, abstract and exploratory. Whether the particular clock-cover concept directly illustrates a musical passage is an interpretation; no original 1964 art-director interview for this sleeve was examined.

## INTERPRETATION, ALTERNATIVE, AND HISTORICAL LIMIT
**Hypothesis:** a surprising content-specific image can act as a meaningful metaphor, while precise typographic information anchors the artist/record facts. **Alternative:** the cover’s value may be primarily linked to historic jazz language and the LP as a tangible album object. Its surreal signage should not be copied as a novelty UI pattern.

## CONDITIONAL DIGITAL TRANSFER
**Use when:** artist identity/marketing wants a strong *authentic* image tied to an actual song/show/story, supported by a coherent typographic hierarchy.

**Don't use when:** fabricating visual metaphors about an artist without permission, substituting stylized art for recognizably functioning controls, making a false “will be back” UI status, or borrowing classic Blue Note visual identity wholesale.

## ORIGINAL EXERCISE
Choose an artist's **verified real song, photo, and approved words**. Develop three editorial identities that differ in meaning (not three filters): typography-led artifact; photographic document; abstract material memory. At 320/390px keep artist name, actual content/media links and task actions readable. Record artist rights, photo credits, crop, mobile reflow, and where it should become restrained utility UI. If authentic assets are unavailable, brief the desired shoot rather than invent a documentary photograph.

**Evidence class:** Blue Note primary label history + secondary collector image thumbnail. **Study scope:** one album sleeve preview, not a survey of label's 500 covers; **UX outcomes NOT RUN**.
