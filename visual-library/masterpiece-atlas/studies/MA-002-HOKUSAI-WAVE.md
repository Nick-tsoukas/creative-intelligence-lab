# MA-002 — Scale, rhythm and a stable distant anchor: Hokusai, *Under the Wave off Kanagawa*

- **Creator / date / medium:** Katsushika Hokusai, c. 1830–32, woodblock print, ink and color on paper, from *Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji*.
- **Original museum object:** The Metropolitan Museum of Art, JP10, https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/36491. Exact full title there: *Under the Wave off Kanagawa (Kanagawa oki nami ura)*. Met marks its image **Public Domain**.
- **Image evidence:** Met's own preview/thumbnail visibly inspected 2026-11-15. The tool-rendered view appears fit/cropped to a square preview relative to the original landscape sheet; **do not claim complete plate boundaries/color accuracy**. Original print is 24.4 × 35.7 cm per museum.
- **Rights:** Met public-domain object image; still verify reproduction method and credit policy if later exporting media; not committed in repo.

## Observed preview (limited)
A towering dark-blue curling wave and sharp white foam articulate a large upper-left/central mass. Small narrow boats angle through the foreground waves; a modest triangular mountain appears distant at lower right. The visible print juxtaposes a spectacular, rhythmically repeating near form with a much smaller, stable background landmark. Blue contours and foam clusters repeat at changing scales.

## Museum context
Met catalog records this as a woodblock edition within a landscape series, dated ca. 1830–32, and identifies Mount Fuji in the composition. The printed medium, multiple copies and woodblock practices matter: this is not a unique oil painting, and one museum impression may differ in condition from another.

## Interpretation / alternative
**Hypothesis:** strong foreground disruption against a visually constant background reference can create drama while preserving spatial legibility. **Alternative:** Fuji's significance is deeply tied to this original cultural subject/series, not a universal "small anchor improves interfaces" principle.

## Transferable mechanism
**Use when:** an editorial/landing section should contrast the intensity of a lived moment against a quieter, enduring relationship or object. Borrow **scale contrast and repetition**, not an identifiable wave silhouette.

**Don't use when:** app controls, text, forms, fan consent or errors would be overpowered by a graphic wave; never assume spectacle inherently increases conversion.
