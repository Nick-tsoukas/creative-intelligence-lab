# Research and Evidence Protocol

## Provenance tags
- **FACT / VERIFIED:** directly supported by original source with durable citation.
- **OBSERVATION:** first-hand description of a specific image, artifact or interaction; list viewing conditions.
- **PRACTITIONER VIEW:** attributed idea or preference from a designer, studio or scholar.
- **INTERPRETATION:** our critical reading, not a claim about author intent.
- **HYPOTHESIS:** an original or contested claim with proposed falsification or test.
- **UNVERIFIED:** source unavailable, stale, or observation untested.

## Source metadata
Record title, creator, publication year when known, URL/DOI, access date, source type, source status (QUEUED, LINK-VERIFIED, EXCERPT-REVIEWED, PARTIALLY-READ, FULLY-READ, DIRECTLY-OBSERVED, NEEDS-RECHECK), and constraints.

## Research entry structure
1. Question and why it matters.
2. Specific primary artifact(s), exact references and inspection scope.
3. Documented facts with citations.
4. Original visual observations (composition, hierarchy, color, typography, image, motion).
5. UX and accessibility implications (do not infer audits from screenshots).
6. Counterexamples or competing interpretations.
7. Transferable principles **with conditions and failure modes**.
8. Follow-up experiments / unresolved questions.

## Quality gates
- No invented quotations, page numbers, performance numbers, technologies, or awards.
- No universal laws from attractive examples; the golden ratio is not a magic constant for beauty.
- Never confuse popularity with excellence or UX conformance with artistic originality.
- Verify source date and current product behavior for time-sensitive design trends.
- Keep copyrighted visual references as links unless use is authorized.
- Test across mobile, low-powered hardware and reduced-motion environments when implementing.
- Clearly mark aesthetics as interpretive rather than scientific certainty.

## Writing protocol
Prefer a few rigorous case studies to long unexamined lists. Each substantive change should update ROADMAP, STATUS or CHANGELOG as appropriate. Avoid duplicating whole documents; use links.
