**Source:** Abby Covert, *How to Make Sense of Any Mess*, author's [complete authorized web edition](https://www.howtomakesenseofanymess.com/), with [author's confirmation of free complete online access](https://abbycovert.com/make-sense/). Web edition notes ©2022; verify print edition separately.

The seven-chapter progression was inspected as a structure map, not the entire prose.

## Observed author's argument
- A “mess” is not merely a bad screen: it can be the structure of people, activities and communication relationships. Thus a UI rearrangement may fail because the underlying conceptual map remains unclear.
- Declared **intent is shaped by language**. If two actors use the same word differently, interface labels can cement a confusing category even when visual presentation is polished.
- An early architecture diagram should use **objects and relationships**, kept cheap and revisable. Covert suggests manipulating boxes and connections before visual styling makes it emotionally harder to revise.

## Transferred hypothesis (not a user-study result)
For any recurring Faneqo-like “I don't know where my Night/gallery/message lives” complaint: first list actor verbs, their objects and relationships; build a minimal concept diagram; then make the shortest visible task path. Don't flatten genuinely different permission/visibility relationships merely to reduce words.

For homepage work: distinguish visitor-oriented product vocabulary (what a user wants to do) from internal data-model nouns. A cinematic message that does not reveal the product's role may have a **vocabulary/architecture** problem, not a font-size problem.

## Counterexamples and failure guards
A box diagram is a communication aid, not proof of understanding. A highly specialized audience may already know domain terminology; don't ban precise vocabulary. Discovered relationships must be verified against backend truth and consent/roles, not decided only from a diagram.

## Candidate cards
- **DK-014A:** Before restructuring navigation, diagram user nouns/verbs and relationships; conditional on evidence of cross-surface conceptual confusion.
- **DK-014B:** Treat labeling as a product-model decision, not a late visual polish step; conditional on unclear audience vocabulary.

Both candidates require real route/screen evidence to be applied; no actual Faneqo testing was performed for this dossier.
