## Selected passage A — active line as a moving point (I.1)

Klee's 1925 original German opens the line section with the idea that an active line goes freely on a walk; the point displaces to generate movement. The online English excerpt describes a freely moving line that need not have a predetermined destination.

**Original text anchor:** Section I.1; original source excerpt in Bauhaus Bookshelf German edition. **Translation note:** English wording in online selection attributed to Sibyl Moholy-Nagy (1953).

**Close reading:** The point is not merely an inert dot and the line not merely an outline; their relationship makes motion perceivable. Form is narrated as *a process of becoming*. This is an expressive model, not an empirical claim about where today's viewers actually look.

**Creative transfer:** Treat typographic baselines, image edges and section connections as traces of visual movement. The design should give an intentional route of attention without locking the user into one reading path.

**False transfer:** Automatically animate every drawn line, make content appear in mandatory order, or use ornamental path graphics as a substitute for music culture.

## Selected passage B — circulation and transformation (I.12)

The excerpt maps a circulatory system through three roles: pumping action (heart), transmitted movement (blood), and transformation/participation (lungs). The same system returns to its origin.

**Close reading:** Klee uses a biological analogy to distinguish forces and roles within a system. The system communicates through relations, not identical treatment of all parts.

**Creative transfer:** Distinguish initiating action, supporting evidence, and feedback result in visual communication (for example: artist intent → performance evidence → audience participation → artist growth). A diagram of connected roles may clarify an experience better than a set of equal-priority cards.

**Risk:** Human biological metaphors need not describe product architecture. This is a possible storytelling analogy, not proof of interface behavior.

## Selected passage C — asymmetrical balance (II, German chapter 'Nicht symmetrisches Gleichgewicht')

The German contents explicitly include non-symmetrical equilibrium. Sibyl Moholy-Nagy's **introduction**, not Klee's direct text, explains equilibrium as equalization of unequal-but-comparable parts.

**Close reading:** Balance does not have to be reflection around a center. The viewer may experience two unlike masses as composing a coherent whole. But whether a particular image is 'balanced' remains a context-sensitive artistic judgment.

**Creative transfer:** Pair a large musician portrait with a comparatively smaller yet typographically authoritative headline/action cluster. Test crop, whitespace, contrast and the role of each side rather than mechanically forcing a symmetrical split.

**Risk:** Asymmetry can become arbitrary if important content is lost at 320px or visual cues do not communicate their purpose.

## Selected passage D — eye movement and the path within the work

The accessible excerpt says the eye moves through pathways established by the composition (the source includes a short line and its translation). This is a **historical artistic observation**, not a validated eye-tracking prediction for all viewers.

**Transfer:** Art direction can deliberately suggest arrival, discovery, verification, and action; the website still needs direct navigation and accessible non-linear routes.

**Counterexample:** scrolljacking or very slow cinematic reveals that force visitors through the creator's narrative before they can find pricing or information.

## Selected passage E — kinetic forms, arrow, circular systems (IV)

The original table of contents includes turning, pendulum, circle, spiral, arrow and movement-organism topics; English excerpt from IV.40 treats the arrow in symbolic terms. Klee examines the difference between a directional intention and the forces that hinder or redirect motion.

**Transfer:** Motion should make state transitions legible (opening, closing, entering, leaving, changing status). Use purposeful motion vectors instead of arbitrary easing chosen because it looks 'premium'.

**Counterexample:** adding traveling arrows or glowing waveforms throughout a music application without linking to content or interaction state.

## Important artistic conclusion

Klee offers something more valuable than a recipe for Bauhaus graphics: he teaches us to describe visual form in terms of *generating forces*—paths, contrasts, transformations, equilibrium and directions. The Lab can apply that mode of thinking while rejecting literal imitations of Klee's drawings.
