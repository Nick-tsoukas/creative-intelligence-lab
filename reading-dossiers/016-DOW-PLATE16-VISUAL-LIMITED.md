# Visual Dossier 016 — Dow's Plate No. 16: Subordination (Partial Reproduction)

**Source work:** Arthur Wesley Dow, *Composition*, specifically the original discussion “Ways of Creating Harmony”, “Subordination”, and the reference to **No. 16**, in the verified **1914 ninth-edition** Gutenberg text: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45410 and [original page text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45410/45410-h/45410-h.htm).

**Visual evidence actually inspected 2026-11-15:** A **cropped, approximately 512-pixel** photograph of an illustrated printed page, embedded in Stephen Berry's 2019 [reading guide](https://stephenberryart.com/blog/2019/1/17/a-reading-guide-to-dows-composition-pt-2-principles-of-composition-and-good-spacing), photo titled `IMG_5368.JPG` ([image link](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/54f4b069e4b0c90de2c95ea4/1549058178135-72GLJOL6NK8NJQ34U2X2/IMG_5368.JPG)). The **full original scanned plate and physical copy's edition were not established** from that crop. No full book or image set has been visually studied.

## OBSERVED (only in the visible crop)
- The scanned fragment has a heading at left corresponding to “Principles of Composition III”.
- An architectural-looking repeated ornamental arch, with dark curling shapes, is organized around a vertical line. A caption below visibly identifies “Subordination to an Axis”.
- Near it, a branch-like angular drawing shows a dominant trunk with smaller protrusions, cropped at its right.
- The lower area contains a cluster of gabled buildings/roofs, with a larger central gable and smaller adjacent shapes. The adjacent handwritten caption says “Subordination by Size”.
- A petal/radiating motif appears **partly clipped** at the right edge; **do not claim to know the full motif or geometry**.

## SOURCE SAYS (separate from seeing)
Dow's corresponding text describes principal-subordinate organization by **axis, radiation, size** and says merely arranging large/small shapes is insufficient without judgment of spacing and proportion. This description comes from the source prose, not from visual inspection of all three example figures.

## INTERPRETATION (explicitly tentative)
In the cropped ornamental drawing, repeated small forms appear visually legible because a larger shared structural direction binds them. In the building scene, a dominant roof mass relates to visibly smaller neighbor masses. This is a different purpose from putting a single bright CTA in a modern website, and neither figure provides direct evidence for webpage task success.

## MODERN TRANSFER HYPOTHESIS
If a real product homepage's headings, imagery, feature snippets and CTAs compete at equal weight, experiment with one *semantically justified* dominant content relationship and subordinate information. Maintain access to genuinely coequal tasks; test at actual 320/390px render width; check focus order and first-exposure comprehension. This is **not** a requirement that every page have one visual center.
