## Original question
Who defines a design problem, who counts as a knowledgeable designer, who is affected by a system, and who gets durable decision-making power? The author argues that conventional UCD language may include users as consulted subjects while design priorities remain controlled by others.

## Three distinct ideas from the examined original sections
1. **Unvalidated personas can reproduce designer assumptions (personas/mental-model discussion).** An attractive persona template is no replacement for direct knowledge. The author notes that fictional demographic portraits may falsely suggest genuine inclusion. The mechanism matters even for nonpolitical software: an AI-generated “touring artist persona” can conceal our uncertainty about artists' actual workflows.
2. **Simulated lived experience is not equivalent to participation (disability simulation subsection).** Pretending to be a disabled user can generate distorted assumptions; the chapter favors sustained involvement and accountability, not role-playing as evidence of actual access barriers. Accessibility checks and standards still matter, but are not a substitute for real affected expertise.
3. **Participation is weaker than accountability and ownership (chapter section with that name).** The author explicitly distinguishes including users at moments chosen by designers from affected communities helping determine the project. The account of the #MoreThanCode participatory action research reports 109 practitioner interviews and eleven focus groups with seventy-nine participants. These are source-reported figures for that particular research, not generic UX performance numbers.

## Argument and disagreements
This source offers a normative, political and participatory framework; it should not be misrepresented as a randomized experiment showing every co-design method yields higher conversion. It challenges a strictly expert-led canon (Vignelli/Norman interpreted narrowly) by asking **who has authority** to define “better.” In commercial musician tools, time/compensation constraints may limit formal co-ownership, but that does not justify guessing about artist or fan preferences or describing founder/agent judgment as validated research.

## Faneqo-specific application, proposed only
**Artist task:** “After the concert, tell people who were there that photos are ready.” **Fan concern:** “I should understand if a message is private, sent to all followers, or tied to an event, and whether my attendance or gallery is visible.” Both sides must inform the requirements.

Instead of assuming one founder/designer story, create separate evidence columns:
- **Founder report:** current composer feels confusing, gallery privacy inconsistent; distinguish this real report from measured frequency.
- **Artist testimony to obtain later:** would they enter from Night, Messages, or Gallery? What term describes their audience?
- **Fan testimony to obtain later:** how do they expect photos, permissions and opt-ins to behave?
- **Engineering reality:** eligibility, limits, delivery status, public/private boundaries from inspected backend.
- **Unresolved governance questions:** who can publish a photo, retract it, or see attendance; who decides defaults; what data is disclosed?

## Counterexamples and caution
Participatory work does not remove the need for engineering constraints or privacy/security expertise; one interview cannot speak for all artists. A beautiful yet technically inaccessible preview cannot be redeemed simply by a consultation meeting. The author critiques power in design broadly; **do not claim this specific chapter supports any exact Faneqo privacy policy or default audience size**.

## Skill translation
- **Elite UX Director:** add an “Affected voices and evidence gaps” field. Never call AI-created personas user research; require affected-person feedback before claiming a contested behavior has been validated. Distinguish privacy assumptions from user-approved rules.
- **Creative Design Director:** avoid a single generic “music fan/artist” aesthetic. Research real genre, culture and participants' own visual language; state who selected/approved artistic direction.
- **Source system:** classify testimony vs documented policy vs expert judgment vs hypothesis. Never inflate stakeholder review into participatory research.

## Cross-source synthesis
Boulton's brief/research chapters describe disciplined inquiry; Design Justice presses on **who controls inquiry**. HCI 2025 studies human use and contexts; Costanza-Chock adds accountability. Frost can standardize components, but standardization must not erase marginalized users' needs.
