## Thesis 1 — Art education as active discovery
**Documented:** In 1934 Albers opposes formulaic craft instruction and foregrounds learning through manipulating material, examining texture, structure and construction with deliberately simple materials. Rather than ask students to imitate an example, he has them find previously unused possibilities.

**Interpretation:** A design system's best teaching unit may be a *controlled experiment*, not a prescriptive "best practices" slide. Design judgment emerges by examining variables.

**Application:** When preparing a visual direction, change ONE thing at a time for some studies—contrast, scale, crop, light, shape—and document the perceived effect. This is not a replacement for holistic composition; it isolates causes before recombining them.

## Thesis 2 — Color is relational, not a palette swatch
**Documented:** His 1954 report explicitly connects perceived color with interacting color, form, quantity and placement. He describes exercises making the same physical color appear different in changing contexts.

**Interpretation:** Token libraries alone do not teach color use. Brand purple can have different perceived salience on dark, warm, gray and highly saturated backgrounds.

**Application:** Create comparative panels with identical accent and label while manipulating only surroundings. Always distinguish *perceived* prominence from WCAG measured contrast; both matter.

## Thesis 3 — Material choice changes visual thinking
**Documented:** Albers's 1939 essay treats material construction as an investigation of firmness, flexibility, texture and form, and warns against reducing design to mechanical craft habits.

**Interpretation:** Digital 'materials' have affordances too—pixel density, scroll space, type rendering, transparent layers, animation, interactive state. Their properties should influence form rather than simply receive a glossy style after the layout is decided.

**Application:** Consider whether an interface concept should be derived from documentary photographs, text, audio traces, maps, data or physical texture. Use subject-specific media; avoid manufactured materiality without meaning.

## What Albers did NOT prove for our purposes
- That all users perceive contextual colors the same way.
- That Albers's visual effects can replace formal contrast standards.
- That a minimal or geometric aesthetic is inherently superior.
- That following his exercises yields a particular conversion uplift.

## Studio exercise — A three-variable color laboratory
1. Create 4 panels, same button fill and type; vary background lightness/temperature only.
2. Ask 3+ observers which panel makes the action appear most important; record disagreement.
3. Evaluate actual WCAG text/control contrast and keyboard focus separately.
4. Repeat with one alternative label and identical layout. Do not confuse subjective perception with task success.
5. Record hypotheses, observations, counterexamples, and what changed.
