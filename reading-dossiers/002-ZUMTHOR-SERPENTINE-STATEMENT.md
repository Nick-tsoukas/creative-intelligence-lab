# Reading Dossier 002 — Peter Zumthor on an Enclosed Garden

**Date:** 2026-11-11. **Text read:** Zumthor's short May 2011 architect statement, as published by the commissioning Serpentine Galleries, plus accompanying institutional project description.
**Source:** https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/serpentine-gallery-pavilion-2011-peter-zumthor/
**Status:** ORIGINAL-ARCHITECT-STATEMENT-REVIEWED. Not a full book, site visit or reading of his *Atmospheres*.

## Context
The 2011 Serpentine Pavilion, created by Zumthor with garden designer Piet Oudolf, used an enclosed interior garden as its organizing idea. The visitor enters from an open lawn and moves through enclosing architecture to an interior refuge.

## Author's concept (summary, not a direct quotation)
Zumthor describes the garden as a close, protected space in which people nurture plants. He returns to the experience of gardens enclosed by walls or buildings. His desired garden is surrounded, intimate, and open to the sky.

## Analysis — the emotional mechanism
**Interpretation:** The power is not that a wall is dark or a garden is beautiful; it is that *enclosure changes the way a subsequent opening feels.* The distinction between public city and sheltered interior makes attention and emotion sharper.

### Transferable questions
- What environmental contrast is meaningful for this audience?
- What happens before and after the transition?
- Does the experience create a legitimate feeling of discovery or an artificial delay?
- Can the user bypass the threshold to perform a task?
- Is the desired atmosphere supported by authentic materials/content?
