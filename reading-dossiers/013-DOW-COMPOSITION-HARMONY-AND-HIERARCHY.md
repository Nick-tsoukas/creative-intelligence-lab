## What Dow actually argues
Dow rejects training that only imitates finished works; he proposes making and critiquing **relationships** of lines, masses of dark and light (**notan**) and colors. He sees composition as cultivated judgment about spacing, not a magic mathematical recipe. In his introductory structure, line organizes boundaries and spaces, notan concerns massed value relationships rather than literal lighting effects, and color interacts with both. He advises cycling among these elements in progressive exercises, instead of dwelling indefinitely on one.

In *Ways of Creating Harmony* he names **opposition**, **transition**, **subordination**, **repetition** and **symmetry** as ways to relate elements, under a broader judgment of good spacing/proportion. This is a historical pedagogical model—not a demonstrated universal scientific taxonomy of beauty.

## Specific mechanism and countermechanism
- **Subordination:** parts gain coherence by belonging to a single leading element, by axes, radiation or relative size; simple changes in one element alter the whole. A UX transfer would be choosing one genuinely primary user question/action and organizing supporting content around it.
- **Repetition:** repeating a component or rhythm can connect parts, but Dow explicitly warns that mere repetition without deliberate spacing is not art. Applied to UI: a pile of equal-looking cards is not coherent merely because every card uses the same token.
- **Opposition vs transition:** contrast supplies structure; intermediary relation may soften an overly abrupt change. UX analogy: separate major product chapters yet make transitions legible. *This is interpretation*, not evidence that a particular scroll animation will work.
- **Notan:** examine large dark/light masses before fine colors. Digital transfer: assess whether images, labels and important CTA maintain relative value hierarchy even if accents vanish. Do not equate historical notan with a WCAG contrast check; both are different analyses.

## Proposed diagnostic exercise (not performed on Faneqo)
1. Take a real homepage section, grayscale the **rendered screenshot** and identify the dominant mass, visual route and supporting details. Record the observed contrast arrangement before inventing artistic intent.
2. Draw three one-minute massing thumbnails preserving exact user content/action: one with a single dominant content object, one with a clear split/opposition and one with a controlled repeating rhythm.
3. Test first-view comprehension and readable actions at mobile and desktop sizes; keep any art approach only if it supports task and culture.
4. Recheck real focus order, text contrast and responsive reflow independently. Historical compositional harmony alone is not accessibility compliance.

## Knowledge candidates
- **DK-013A — Subordination with task priority**: WHEN multiple equal-weight UI elements compete, organize them under the user's dominant immediate job; COUNTEREXAMPLE high-density expert displays may legitimately use multiple simultaneous focal regions. Source: Dow, 1914, Section III printed pp. 23–24; application is original interpretation, untested.
- **DK-013B — Repetition requires spacing judgment**: WHEN a design appears generic despite consistent components, look at figure-ground space and meaningful variation; COUNTEREXAMPLE repeated equal weights can accurately represent genuinely equivalent options. Source: Section III printed pp. 24–28; interpretation, untested.
- **DK-013C — Notan before color decoration**: WHEN tonal hierarchy is unclear, compare large-value pattern before choosing accents; COUNTEREXAMPLE brand hues and semantic colors carry meaning not captured by grayscale. Source: The Three Elements, printed pp. 7–9; interpretation, untested.
