# Research Chapter 002 — The Invisible Geometry of Beauty

**Status:** Preliminary synthesis from previous conversations; original citations and source interpretations require independent verification before promoting to a definitive research chapter.

## Question
Why can simple geometric structures produce such different responses—drama, equilibrium, rhythmic intensity, and stillness—without a universal mathematical beauty formula?

## Four studies
**Leonardo, The Last Supper:** Architectural convergence and grouped human figures provide order for emotional narrative. Interpretative principle: structural stability can contrast with expressive activity.

**Piet Mondrian, Composition No. I with Red and Blue (1931):** Rectangular planes, asymmetry, and revised relationships express equilibrium without mechanical symmetry. Investigate documentary reports of underlying charcoal revisions.

**Josef Müller-Brockmann, Beethoven poster (1955):** Interrupted concentric rings and disciplined typography make repetition and variation visually suggest music.

**Tadao Ando, Church of the Light (1989):** Spatial enclosure, concrete, and a cruciform light opening create powerful contrast. Architectural experience unfolds in time, not a fixed image.

## Empirical cautions
Golden ratio is a mathematical constant, not an established universal law of aesthetic preference. Visual attention, balance, fluency, preference and memorability are different outcomes. Avoid fitting lines or rectangles to a finished composition and claiming the artist designed with that ratio absent documentation.

## Proposed synthesis (hypothesis, not validated science)
Five forces: order, contrast, rhythm, space and meaning. These can co-operate without any one fixed proportion guaranteeing beauty.
