## Six master domains

### 1. Painting and drawing — how images command attention
Study original works with actual visual inspection: Leonardo da Vinci (Mona Lisa/Last Supper), Vermeer (spatial light), Caravaggio (dramatic illumination), Hokusai (line, gesture and wave composition), Rothko (color fields), Klee and Kandinsky (form/visual rhythm), Mondrian (relational balance). Avoid universal golden-ratio claims absent original measured evidence.

**Questions:** Where is the first eye stop? How is depth established? What is subordinated? What does omission accomplish? How does the work hold attention for a second reading?

**Transfer:** strong hero focal points; purposeful hierarchy; controlled background contrast; only when clear tasks remain visible.

### 2. Graphic design, typography and editorial
Study Johnston, Morris, Tschichold, Müller-Brockmann, Ruder, Rand, Scher, Brody, Saville, Carson, Lupton, Bringhurst, Dondis. Use the actual work and designer's own interviews/archives, not copycat moodboards.

**Questions:** What is the typographic voice? What grid/anti-grid governs rhythm? What stays consistent across a visual system? What tension creates identity?

**Transfer:** Faneqo identity vocabulary; concert recap editorial composition; readable messaging utility type contrasted with expressive marketing typography.

### 3. Photography and cinematography
Study original photographer portfolios and film scenes where accessible: Sugimoto (restraint/time), Crewdson (staged atmosphere), Gordon Parks (narrative/people), concert photographers and authorized artist archives. Study film composition, lens/vantage, focal depth, key/fill/rim light, warm/cool storytelling, montage rhythm, cuts and temporal continuity. Separate original artist statements from our interpretation.

**Transfer:** art-directed show imagery, artist Headquarters, editorial marketing, and meaningful motion; avoid fake concert photos presented as real artists.

### 4. Architecture, space and sequence
Study Ando, Zumthor, Le Corbusier, contemporary installation/scenography and exhibition designers. Examine entrance, thresholds, compression/release, light, negative space and spatial narrative—not literal architectural styling.

**Transfer:** page sections as sequenced attention; transitions between show moment and fan relationship; strong entrance rather than a feature-card wall.

### 5. Interaction, motion and computational media
Study digital art direction and award-winning live sites through **actual page interaction and device recordings when available**, not award badges alone. Research WebGL, 3D, CSS and canvas as artistic media only if performance/accessibility budgets permit. Distinguish site-of-the-day jury taste from representative user performance.

**Transfer:** movement must clarify meaning, deepen emotional rhythm or reveal content; never obscure critical navigation, freeze scrolling or rely on animation for comprehension.

### 6. Music culture and real artist identity
Study iconic music artwork, gig posters, album systems, stage photography, tour visuals and authentic musician stories. Analyze genre signals without stereotyped templates. Respect artist licensing, photography rights, text accuracy and diversity across career/genre stages.

**Transfer:** Faneqo's actual difference: an artist's world persists after the show; make pages feel like real music culture without compromising service tools.

## Art-study dossier standard

For each selected art object or digital work, require:
- Exact artwork title, creator, date, medium, institution/project URL, image permissions and **directly inspected visual evidence**.
- **Description before interpretation:** concrete arrangement, light, type, crop, rhythm, scale, color relations, apparent emphasis, motion if present.
- Art-historical context and direct artist/designer statements where available; identify uncertainty and conflicting readings.
- A 1-sentence artistic mechanism and an explicit **false transfer** to avoid.
- Two original composition exercises with one controlled variable (e.g., same headline/imagery; change focal weight or crop).
- A specific UI design opportunity for Faneqo, naming purpose, persona, device, task and constraints.
- A counterexample where that principle harms usability.
- Status: REFERENCE IDENTIFIED / WORK VIEWED / SOURCE CONTEXT READ / COMPARATIVE STUDY / EXPERIMENTED / USED IN REAL DESIGN / OWNER ACCEPTED.

## Creative Director methodology upgrade

**Meaning before visual treatment.** Begin from audience, artist identity and emotional/business job. Write a **creative thesis** e.g. “The performance is temporary; the connection continues.” Not 'black/purple gradients'.

**Artistic grammar.** Define 3–5 distinctive rules: focal composition, typographic voice, light/image policy, spacing/rhythm, and transition behavior. Each must have examples, exceptions, and responsive implications.

**Three distinct concepts.** Vary conceptual premise, visual narrative and photographic/typographic roles. Avoid three recolored versions of the same template.

**Source-aware originality.** Inspect real references, extract transferable relationships, and reject distinctive lookalikes. Avoid pretending that striking art mechanically causes conversions.

**Art-director critique.** Separate five independent judgments: (1) artistic concept, (2) hierarchy and meaning, (3) cultural/source authenticity, (4) task usability/accessibility, (5) craft and technical feasibility. Record clear observed evidence and improvement plan; don't collapse them to a fake single 'beauty score'.

**Responsive composition.** A meaningful visual concept must survive 320, 390, 768 and 1440 widths, motion preference, image failure, long copy and authentic media. Expressive marketing and utility messaging may demand different visual intensity.

**Image direction.** Specify subject, environmental truth, camera vantage, light, negative space, crops and fallback, before stock-photo search or generative imagery. For real artist portraits and logos, use authorized authentic assets.

**Deliverable.** A short direction packet plus precise inspiration mechanisms, asset/crop notes, distinctive concept variants, and a post-implementation visual critique—rather than a 50-page essay.
