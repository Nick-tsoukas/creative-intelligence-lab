# Research 010 — Architecture of Digital Experience

**Research date:** 2026-11-11. Sources: architect foundation/commissioning institution, architectural journalism, and UX research documentation. No site visit, film study, visitor interviews, screenshots, or empirical experiment conducted.

## Question
Can we learn to design *sequences of understanding* from architects without turning the web into an exhausting linear tour?

## Three distinctions
- **A view** is what the person sees at one moment.
- **A path** is how the person moves between views.
- **A task** is what the person intends to accomplish, which may require skipping the designed path.

An architectural promenade is useful as a storytelling analogy but cannot dictate a web visitor's route. On the web, freedom to jump, search and return is often crucial.

## Case 1 — Villa Savoye: unfolding frames
**Creator:** Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret. **Primary institutional source:** Fondation Le Corbusier: https://www.fondationlecorbusier.fr/en/work-architecture/achievements-villa-savoye-and-gardeners-lodge-poissy-france-1928-1931/
**Evidence status:** INSTITUTIONAL-PROJECT-TEXT-REVIEWED; not a firsthand building observation.

**Documented:** Built 1928–1931. The foundation describes an *architectural promenade*: circulation is organized so successive frames and viewpoints emerge through movement, with ramp and stairs providing alternative means of circulation. The house also uses architectural polychromy and integration with the landscape.

**Interpretation:** The experience isn't delivered as one picture; perception unfolds as a relationship between movement, frame and vantage. The dual paths matter: although a sequence is composed, a visitor need not always take the same path.

**Digital translation:** A long-form experience could proceed through orientation → central story → evidence → exploration → action, but it should preserve immediate navigation, a skip path and direct links to useful destinations.

**Potential failure:** Scrolljacking, fixed-duration reveals, or forcing visitors to see cinematic sections before reaching pricing/navigation.

**Test:** Present the same content as (a) continuous editorial page, (b) sequential reveal with free navigation, (c) forced reveal. Compare information retrieval, task completion, comprehension and user control. The expected preference is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Case 2 — Church of the Light: one luminous event
**Architect:** Tadao Ando. **Source:** ArchDaily, Andrew Kroll (2011): https://www.archdaily.com/101260/ad-classics-church-of-the-light-tadao-ando
**Additional architectural interpretation:** https://www.archdaily.com/915270/when-sunlight-meets-tadao-andos-concrete
**Evidence status:** ARCHITECTURAL-PROJECT-ARTICLE-REVIEWED; secondary contextual account, not verified original architectural drawings or physical visit.

**Documented:** Original church completed 1989 as part of a later-completed complex. A cruciform opening introduces daylight into a largely unadorned concrete volume. The article discusses opposing conditions—light/dark, solid/void—and precision in the concrete construction. Note: a common article metadata 'Year 1999' describes the wider work; the church's first completion is documented as 1989.

**Interpretation:** Contrast is most meaningful when the entire environment supports its central event. The dramatic aperture could not have equivalent impact against competing ornament.

**Digital translation:** Create one dominant moment rather than many simultaneous heroes, and make secondary UI calm. Brightness itself is not the design; **the relationship between brightness, context, and purpose** is.

**Potential failure:** Excessively dark interfaces with low-contrast utility text, inaccessible overlays, and theatrical emptiness that conceals navigation.

## Case 3 — Serpentine Pavilion 2011: threshold and refuge
**Architect:** Peter Zumthor; garden designer Piet Oudolf.
**Primary commissioning institution:** https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/serpentine-gallery-pavilion-2011-peter-zumthor/
**Evidence status:** INSTITUTIONAL-DESCRIPTION-AND-ARCHITECT-STATEMENT-REVIEWED; not a visit.

**Documented:** The pavilion's notion was an enclosed garden. The institutional page explains movement from the exterior lawn through an enclosing structure to a contemplative interior garden. Zumthor's May 2011 statement emphasizes care, protection, enclosure, intimacy, and contrast with the surrounding city.

**Interpretation:** Emotion can emerge through *the difference between consecutive environments*. The pavilion gives urban noise and an interior sanctuary a deliberate relationship.

**Digital translation:** Design thresholds where the user's mental mode changes: information → exploration, promotion → trust, activity → reflection. The typography, light, rhythm and motion can change across the threshold while navigation remains familiar.

**Potential failure:** Unnecessary splash screens, hidden content, or slow transitions that reduce user agency.

## UX counterweight — visual hierarchy isn't a movie storyboard
**Nielsen Norman Group:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/
The article identifies scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast and Gestalt as major visual principles. The article was last reviewed September 1, 2026. This is practitioner guidance, not an empirical proof that any specific cinematic technique increases conversion.

**Our synthesis:** Treat a website as a navigable environment, *not* as a single imposed path. Support recognition rather than recall: visible headings, accessible navigation, and clear actions.

## Five architecture-derived decision mechanisms
| Mechanism | Artistic source | Product use | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sequential frame | Villa Savoye | reveal evidence chapter by chapter | forced navigation |
| Dominant aperture | Church of Light | one strong content/CTA focal event | low legibility |
| Threshold | Serpentine Pavilion | purposeful emotional shift between sections | splash-screen friction |
| Material consistency | Ando/Zumthor contexts | one coherent image/typographic environment | generic borrowed materials |
| Alternate circulation | Villa Savoye | story path plus skip/search/direct destination | inaccessible or hidden shortcuts |

## Design exercise — one page, three architectural structures
Use the same fictional product story and copy. Draw:
1. **Promenade:** six sections with visible global navigation and section jumps.
2. **Single aperture:** restrained page with one concentrated hero/primary action.
3. **Threshold:** opening atmosphere followed by an immediate, utilitarian catalog.

Before choosing, test where people look for a specific answer and whether they can bypass narrative.
