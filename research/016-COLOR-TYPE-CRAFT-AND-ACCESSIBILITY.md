# Research 016 — Color, Typography and Art Direction Under Real Interface Constraints

## Practical conditional grammar: color
- **When imagery/background varies** (gallery or real artist photography), define *semantic* color roles (foreground/body/action/error/success/focus) independently of artistic color atmospheres. A hero may be warm, cool, muted or electric; the actual controls must retain visible contrast and distinguishable meaning.
- **When everything feels uniformly saturated**, consider whether color *relations* and area/adjacency have collapsed into an all-over effect. Experiment with constant hues, changing only surrounding surface area and value. **Don't** infer cognitive psychology outcomes from Albers pedagogy without studies.
- **When using dark themes**, inspect blackish edges, translucent overlays, gradients, actual photo crops and image-loading fallback in browser. A solid black brand can coexist with thoughtfully warm artistic photography; avoid treating “dark SaaS” as a universal music identity.
- **Counterexample:** a genuinely semantic warning cannot be demoted to low-contrast artistic restraint. Text contrast and focus requirements are measurable compliance checks, not subject to a designer’s preference.

## Practical conditional grammar: typography
- **Display vs utility:** use display scale/expressive optical spacing in a headline or editorial poster *only when meaning survives real wrapping and keyboard navigation*. Buttons, forms, fee terms, privacy text and error states need predictable readable type. Scher/Blue Note show cultural typographic expression; they do not authorize illegible mobile buttons.
- **Editorial rhythm:** compare exact copy in several line lengths, rag shapes and paragraph groupings; check source name spelling, word breaks, long band names and localization. Avoid universal “perfect line length” numeric claims without task/content evidence.
- **Type as navigation:** font variation cannot substitute for real headings, links, focus, semantics and a correct destination. If a cinematic title is an image, provide actual text and meaningful alternative, not decorative inaccessible copy.
- **Falsifier:** screen at 320/390px and 200% text has clipped words, orphaned/obscured CTA, unreadable utility copy or misordered headings. A poster may be artful and still be unsuitable for a checkout screen.

## Practice: one factual message, three expressions
The Lab's `experiments/004-visual-grammar-studio` holds underlying proposition/call-to-action/destination constant and varies composition rather than marketing content. This supports criticism of hierarchy and meaning. **It does not make controlled experimental results**: no participant task study, aesthetic preference study or conversion effect has been measured.

### Critique order
1. **Truth:** what is this, who for, what can I do; are names, availability, consequences and evidence real?
2. **Hierarchy:** inspect full rendered visual mass, local focus, paragraph contrast, meaningful image crops, visible utility CTA.
3. **Typography:** test 320/390, long names, 200% zoom, text-spacing override and fallback fonts.
4. **Color:** test text and component contrast against actual backgrounds, at multiple visual states; avoid broad assumptions about perceptual emotion.
5. **Motion:** can people navigate and understand when animation is off, blocked, reduced or slow?
6. **Culture:** is the art specific to subjects and real stories, or merely an attractive borrowed style?
7. **Engineering:** measure media load/CLS/rendering if actually tested, with screenshot and task evidence.
