# Art Case Study 002 — Villa Savoye: Designing a Sequence Without Dictating It

**Creator:** Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret.
**Location:** Poissy, France.
**Project:** Villa Savoye (1928–1931).
**Source:** https://www.fondationlecorbusier.fr/en/work-architecture/achievements-villa-savoye-and-gardeners-lodge-poissy-france-1928-1931/
**Evidence:** Official foundation project text reviewed 2026-11-11; photography linked, not copied. No building tour undertaken.

## Source observation
The foundation calls the movement organization an *architectural promenade*. The ramp and stairs allow movement while successive framed views appear. The interior includes color treatment and a relationship to the surrounding park.

## Critical interpretation
A view is intentionally framed by movement through space. The architecture does not communicate solely through a front-facing picture. A designer composes relationships between where a person is, what is visible now, and what comes next.

For web experiences, the parallel is information sequence rather than physical circulation. People may enter a website at any section and should not be forced into a prescribed route.

## Two contradictory impulses
- Composed storytelling can make complexity approachable.
- Direct search/navigation empowers visitors with immediate practical goals.

A strong experience should allow both. This is an original design hypothesis, not an established law derived from a house.

## Conditions for useful transfer
Editorial narrative, museum experience, interactive product demonstrations. Not recommended as forced animation for billing, authentication, dashboards, emergency alerts or transactional forms.

## Thought experiment
Arrange the same six facts into three layouts: navigable linear, freely explorable sections, and modal slide-by-slide. Ask a visitor to retrieve a specific fact, then evaluate task time, backtracking and comprehension. Keep content constant.
