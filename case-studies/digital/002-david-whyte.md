## What the creator documented
**FACT / ATTRIBUTED TO STUDIO:** Immersive Garden describes a digital storytelling experience about poet David Whyte combining poetry, sound, 3D visuals, dynamically rendered watercolor-inspired paintings, custom soundscapes, and WebGL. The studio frames the aim as representing memory and emotion through imagery and immersive narrative.

## Critical interpretation
**INTERPRETATION:** Watercolor is not simply decorative: the studio connects its fluidity to the fluidity of the poetry. Here a visual material is a *metaphor* for content. This is a different mechanism from FinalFinal: rather than making workflow tangible, the goal is to create an embodied reading atmosphere.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Narrative media can strengthen emotional coherence when each media layer reinforces the subject. Added complexity can also drown out literature if sound and visuals compete with the text.

## UX counterpoint and unverified questions
- Is the poem readable without WebGL or sound?
- Does the experience preserve text contrast, semantic structure, keyboard access and screen-reader navigation?
- Are users able to pause, mute, seek or skip content?
- Does long loading interrupt contemplative pacing?
- What happens on low-end mobile hardware and under reduced-motion preferences?

No performance data, accessibility audit or current implementation behavior was verified in this pass.

## Lessons
- Make artistic techniques express properties of the subject itself.
- Consider media as layers of one narrative, not independent effects.
- Design an accessible plain-content route alongside immersion.
- A studio award or claimed emotional effect is not independent evidence of user outcomes.
