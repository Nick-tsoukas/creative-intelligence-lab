# The Great UX & Design Library — Ranked Curriculum and Skill Routing

**Date:** 2026-11-15. **Status:** research plan grounded in confirmed open reading editions, not completed reading or a claim of superiority. Core architecture must remain general-purpose; Faneqo examples are an adapter. Related: [130-candidate acquisition catalog](UX_DESIGN_BOOK_CATALOG_2026-11-15.md), [existing UX source ledger](../skills/elite-ux-director/references/SOURCE_LEDGER.md), [master plan](../docs/UX_DESIGN_INTELLIGENCE_MASTER_PLAN.md), and [new reading dossiers](../reading-dossiers/008-HCI-2025-PERCEPTION.md).

## How the 20 were chosen
Prioritized a modern, comprehensive HCI backbone; freely and lawfully accessible actual original text; balanced UX/visual foundations; decisions that transfer to message/permission and content workflows; and sources demonstrably beyond generic UI trends. This is an **ordering for study**, not an aesthetic/academic ranking of authors. All entries are books, though some are multi-volume works or author-hosted web books. Publisher/catalog previews, articles and standards are separately classified.

## Tier 2 — Advanced Human Factors
- **Available and ready:** HCI Chapters 4 Motor Control, 5 Cognition, 6 Needs and Motivations, 9 Communication, 42 Think-Aloud; OpenStax *Psychology 2e* (selected perception, cognition, learning); William James Ch XI for historical contrast; Dewey *Human Nature and Conduct* for historical context.
- **Acquire/verify:** Card–Moran–Newell *The Psychology of Human-Computer Interaction*; Pashler *The Psychology of Attention*; Neisser *Cognition and Reality*; Gibson *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception*; Kahneman *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. These are not asserted freely accessible.
- **Output:** conditional cards for visual search, recognition, input target affordance, cognitive effort, interruptions and habit; explicitly separate mechanism, context, counterexample and tested status.

## Tier 5 — Historical and Interdisciplinary Foundations
- **Available and ready:** James *Principles of Psychology*; Dewey *Human Nature and Conduct*; Darwin *Expression of the Emotions*; Russell *Analysis of Mind*; Croce *Aesthetic as Science of Expression* (1909 Ainslie translation, historical section abridged); Crane, Johnston, Morris; Blackburn *Art of Illustration*.
- **Critical reading lens:** historical observations and art philosophies can inspire questions. Nineteenth-century physiology, experimental psychology claims, or rigid industrial efficiency principles must never be asserted as contemporary empirical HCI findings. Frederick Winslow Taylor's *Principles of Scientific Management* is included as a **critical foil**, not a user-welfare optimization doctrine.

## Five decision cards ready for selectively loaded references

### Card A: Visual salience is not semantic discoverability
**Mechanism:** HCI Ch3 attention/search/recognition plus user expectations. **When:** user says “I didn't know that icon did that.” **Do:** identify human goal, expected label/location, visibility and recognition separately; preserve text labels where needed. **Do not:** assume larger icon solves meaning. **Status:** literature-grounded hypothesis, no Faneqo usability trial.

### Card B: Shared components are not shared permission models
**Mechanism:** Frost component taxonomy + Lynch/Horton architecture. **When:** private chat and broadcast share a composer. **Do:** reuse primitive controls but make recipient-type constraints and send consequences explicit. **Do not:** make distinct domain objects identical to maximize code reuse. **Status:** synthesis.

### Card C: Meaning must precede geometry
**Mechanism:** Vignelli Semantics/Syntactics + Boulton Brief. **When:** aesthetically impressive page with unclear first action. **Do:** state who speaks, to whom, for what purpose; then organize editorial grid, type, image. **Do not:** blindly transplant Vignelli's print metrics to mobile. **Status:** aesthetic practice, not measured usability law.

### Card D: Consider affected users' authority
**Mechanism:** Costanza-Chock participatory design chapter. **When:** predicting what fans/artists “want” or selecting privacy defaults. **Do:** mark founder report, research observation, participant testimony, validated product policy and inference independently. **Do not:** label simulated persona or a model's opinion as empirical user research. **Status:** ethical method recommendation.

### Card E: Repeated ease must not accelerate harmful action
**Mechanism:** James Habit (historical), HCI perception (current), Keith task-first flow. **When:** familiar repeated broadcast task. **Do:** preserve predictable controls and draft recovery, but require clear high-consequence recipient visibility. **Do not:** auto-send to undisclosed audiences in the name of fewer clicks. **Status:** cautious synthesis.

## Reference architecture for Claude Code

**Do not enlarge `skills/*/SKILL.md` with book summaries.** Keep source library independent. For any real task:
1. Load the *one* role-specific skill for that uncertainty, not both by default. UX = user job/state; Creative = meaning/composition; frontend-design = authorized implementation.
2. Retrieve **2–4 reference cards** from this file or a dossier link. Limit each returned card to source anchor, condition, risk, action and counterexample.
3. Write a task-specific deliverable, such as task/state model, creative direction packet, or review, not generic design rules.
4. Include source confidence, owner approval status, what was observed and what remains to check.
5. Send Faneqo facts via a current domain adapter/glossary, **never hard-code them into universal skill logic**.

Suggested minimal trigger interface in the Lab repo: `UX_OR_ART_DIRECTOR_SOURCE_ROUTER.md` with tagged cases (navigation, messaging, forms, privacy, visual hierarchy, typography, media). This is a documentation recommendation, not code built or an installed skill. Reuse the existing `research/012-AGENT-SKILLS-UX-ARCHITECTURE.md` rather than adding an orchestrator.

## Conflicts worth preserving
- **Vignelli's purposeful ambiguity** vs privacy-critical recipient clarity. Resolution: ambiguity belongs in expressive composition, not in consent/operational outcomes.
- **Frost's cross-screen component reuse** vs Lynch/Horton's domain vocabulary. Resolution: share primitives but preserve distinct semantics.
- **Expert UX guidance** vs Costanza-Chock's community decision rights. Resolution: expert knowledge is evidence, not proof that affected people approve.
- **Attention drawn by contrast** vs expectation-driven search. Resolution: typography, saliency, label and task location all matter.
- **James's habit philosophy** vs contemporary experimental validity. Resolution: keep history as inquiry, use modern corroboration for factual claims.
