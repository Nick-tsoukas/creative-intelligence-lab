# Signal Archive — Three Deliberately Different Directions

**Status:** ART-DIRECTION CANDIDATES. No prototype or user testing completed. The owner has not selected a direction.

## A — Listening Room / restrained editorial
**Thesis:** Every recorded environment deserves a quiet place to be heard.
- **Structure:** small masthead, large elegant headline, sparse location index, crisp list-first catalog with persistent search.
- **Typography:** contrast of modest expressive editorial heading and calm utility text, generous readable measure.
- **Image:** subtle monochrome abstract soundwave diagram; no decorative stock photography.
- **Color:** off-white / charcoal / one vivid highlight for active playback or favorite.
- **Motion:** tiny feedback transitions; no mandatory scroll reveal.
- **Primary task:** immediately browse/search six recordings.
- **Risk:** sophistication could collapse into an undifferentiated minimalist grid. Need an authentic acoustic motif.
- **Reference mechanism:** selective reduction and refined hierarchy; Vermeer and Linear, not visual copying.

## B — Sound Terrain / immersive sensory atlas
**Thesis:** Sounds reveal the emotional geography of a place.
- **Structure:** bold cartographic shapes and a large featured sound; category filters visible beneath the hero; evocative location strips.
- **Typography:** monumental place names paired with restrained metadata.
- **Image:** original abstract terrain/color-field illustration, spatial waveform indicators. Avoid copying real album covers.
- **Color:** deep-night field with warm signal accents and place-specific palettes.
- **Motion:** slow parallax only if functional and optional; waveforms may animate only during actual playback (not faked).
- **Primary task:** feature invites listening, catalogue remains one action away.
- **Risk:** dramatic canvas may delay finding a particular sound; GPU load and legibility at small screens.
- **Reference mechanism:** purposeful light fields and atmosphere (Turrell interpretation), subject-specific visual narrative (David Whyte case).
- **Safeguard:** content and filters usable without canvas/WebGL/motion.

## C — Field Notes / archival experimental hybrid
**Thesis:** Each sound is a found artifact with its own story.
- **Structure:** a notebook-like index with numbered specimen cards and annotated metadata; an editorial opening plus a sturdy search tool.
- **Typography:** confident grotesque labels, a humane serif for narratives, monospaced dates and durations; no tiny annotations.
- **Image:** abstract maps, recording location grids and original signal traces assembled as graphic evidence.
- **Color:** warm paper, ink black, occasional sharp signal orange.
- **Motion:** purposeful reveal of an annotation only on user action, no auto-choreography.
- **Primary task:** search and preview remain visible; each result tells a distinctive story.
- **Risk:** pseudo-scientific labeling could become affectation; too many stamps/lines can add visual noise.
- **Reference mechanism:** documentary hierarchy and rhythmic grid (Müller-Brockmann), distinctive cultural typography (Scher) without copying.

## Comparison table
| Test | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five-second explanation | Likely strong; needs testing | At risk if hero overdominates | Strong if labels kept clear |
| Original identity | Subtle, must be carefully art-directed | Strong but technically expensive | Subject-specific graphic language |
| Keyboard/mobile resilience | Easier baseline | Higher risk | Medium |
| Emotional expression | Reflective | Immersive | Curious/archival |
| First sandbox trial feasibility | High | Medium | High |

## Proposed first implementation
**Recommend C for a first prototype**, not because it is "best," but because it balances an identifiable conceptual metaphor with searchable functional content and creates a real challenge for the skill. Keep A as the UX baseline; consider B as a stress test later. **This is a research recommendation, not owner approval or a direction selected for production.**

## Rejection criteria
If C's archival metaphor creates more visual clutter than the baseline, return to the simpler A direction. If B's immersion hides search or requires GPU-heavy effects, treat that as a design defect rather than art direction triumph.
