# Perceptual Composition Card — Art Director's Review Tool (v0.1)

**Status:** proposed creative direction instrument, not a proven universal perceptual measurement. Companion: [Research 011](../research/011-PERCEPTUAL-ORGANIZATION.md).

## Evaluate one proposed composition, not an entire brand

**Frame:** project/audience / image or screenshot / viewport / intended first action / source reference / observed vs hypothetical.

### The nine questions
1. **First perception:** What does the composition communicate before reading body copy? Record the concrete claim; do not say “impactful.”
2. **Main visual event:** Which element dominates? Is dominance caused by scale, contrast, position, motion, recognizable human subject, or typography?
3. **Asymmetrical balance:** Which element counterweights that dominance? Are we using a productive tension or merely scattering highlights?
4. **Meaningful grouping:** Which labels, buttons and images appear connected? What business/semantic relationship does this grouping assert?
5. **Figure/ground:** Can the primary subject be differentiated immediately? Are necessary controls readable and operable?
6. **Contextual color:** Does an accent still feel appropriate when placed next to photography and adjacent copy? Do not inspect swatches alone.
7. **Rhythm:** Where does the gaze encounter repetition, interruption and pause? Are those transitions meaningful or arbitrary?
8. **Mobile persistence:** At 390px, are the semantic groups, image focal point, and primary action still obvious?
9. **Counterevidence:** Which task could this direction harm? What specific observation would make us abandon or revise it?

## Three-way creative critique

### A useful disagreement protocol
- **Artistic preference**: “I prefer the tension of A.” No claim of usability.
- **Documented source**: “Guggenheim attributes expressive meaning to Kandinsky's circles.” Cite institution and artwork.
- **Observation**: “On the supplied screenshot at 390px, CTA is below hero.” State exact evidence.
- **Tested behavior**: “Participant 4 located CTA after 10 seconds.” Only when participants really exist.
- **Hypothesis**: “B may help recall because the headline and evidence are closer.” Must specify a possible disconfirmation.

## Failure patterns to flag
- Several elements compete for equal visual priority, without a deliberate polyphonic thesis.
- Decorative abstract geometry substitutes for authentic visual content.
- Every section is enclosed in identical cards, flattening information hierarchy.
- Asymmetry is used without meaningful directional tension.
- Deliberate ambiguity obstructs a task (navigation, pricing, signup).
- Black backgrounds, grain and oversize headings are selected because they look “premium,” not because they express the brief.
- Reduced motion, reading order, keyboard focus and mobile behavior are assumed rather than examined.

## Decision output
`Keep / Change / Reject` — one sentence each, plus the test needed. The implementation partner remains free to select technologies, typography details, CSS and interaction mechanics consistent with the approved art direction.
