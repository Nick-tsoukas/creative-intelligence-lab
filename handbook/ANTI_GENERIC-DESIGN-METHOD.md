# The Anti-Generic Design Method — Working Draft 0.1

**Purpose:** build distinctive, emotionally resonant, task-effective digital experiences, not a proprietary visual style or template.
**Status:** Proposed method for testing. Not an established scientific model.

## Why AI-generated interfaces can look interchangeable
**Hypotheses, not measured industry facts:**
- Examples are often drawn from a small set of contemporary landing-page conventions.
- Teams begin with component placement before identifying a concept, story and cultural context.
- Visual decisions become decorative defaults rather than responses to user goals.
- Generic words such as "clean," "premium," or "modern" replace precise visual critique.
- A single plausible direction receives polish before alternatives have been considered.

## Seven-pass creative protocol

### 1. Find the human purpose
Specify audience, goal, environment, risk, frequency of use and desired emotional response. Name functional constraints and the work explicitly out of scope.

### 2. Research outside the category
Examine at least one strong example each from fine art, typography/editorial, cinema/photography, architecture/spatial design, product UX, and a less-familiar cultural tradition. Identify *mechanisms* rather than copying visual signatures.

### 3. Write a conceptual thesis
One sentence expressing what the experience should *mean* or *do* emotionally. Avoid specifying gradients, colors or cards in this sentence. Examples: "calm structure supports explosive creative energy" or "uncovering evidence creates trust." These are invented examples, not product prescriptions.

### 4. Explore opposing visual directions
Develop three substantially different candidates (not hue variations):
- Editorial/typographic restraint.
- Image-led or cinematic expression.
- Subject-specific hybrid concept.
Retain a conventional usability baseline. For each, state a communication strength, UX risk and responsive plan.

### 5. Art-direct relationships, not decoration
Critique scale, balance, whitespace, color context, type anatomy, lighting, image selection/crop, recurring motif, tone and motion rhythm. Remove effects that cannot explain their own purpose.

### 6. Protect interaction contracts
Check semantic controls, keyboard operation, focus visibility, labels, form recovery, user control, responsive reflow, reduced motion and performance. The interactive affordance need not copy a platform's appearance, but its behavior must be understandable.

### 7. Compare and revise
Compare alternatives on independent axes:
- Expressive identity and visual quality.
- Communication clarity and task success.
- Accessibility and engineering quality.
- Relevance to audience and cultural context.
Use critique and actual user tasks; record what has and has not been tested. Do not collapse these axes into one arbitrary "beauty score."

## Two modes of design
**Expressive mode:** a story, showpiece, brand moment or exploratory experience; higher appetite for novelty if meaning remains clear.
**Operational mode:** repetitive tasks, dashboards, data editing, billing, high-stakes decisions; stable structure and predictable behavior often deserve priority.

A product may combine modes in different regions or moments without using two unrelated brands.

## Red flags to challenge
- A template hero with a generic gradient, floating cards, and empty claims.
- Identical rounded cards repeated with no hierarchy.
- Motion applied uniformly to all elements.
- Arbitrary 3D objects unrelated to the content.
- Tiny low-contrast interface text mistaken for elegance.
- Primary actions hidden for the sake of composition.
- Decorative metaphors that cannot be explained in relation to actual users.
- Visual motifs copied intact from admired studios.

## Review questions
1. What could only belong to this audience or subject?
2. What is the strongest single visual decision, and why?
3. Which parts could be removed with no loss of meaning?
4. What would a competitor's generic template look like, and how are we meaningfully different?
5. Can an unfamiliar person perform the essential task?
6. Does the idea survive reduced motion, narrow screens, and a slower device?
7. Which claims are our interpretations rather than documented evidence?
