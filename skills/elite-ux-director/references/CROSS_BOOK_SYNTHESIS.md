# Interdisciplinary Design Synthesis — Conflicts First (v0.5)

Load only when multiple relevant source traditions materially change a design judgment, especially **art direction vs task comprehension, density vs novice clarity, hierarchy vs accessibility, visual storytelling vs accurate product truth, component reuse vs domain semantics**. Do not load for a routine icon/bug fix.

## Method
1. **Restate decision and constraints**: exact person, job, current product behavior, media/environment, approved design doctrine and safety boundaries.
2. Select **two or three directly read sources**, not popular names from a candidate catalog. Write their actual arguments and examined sections.
3. Build a comparison with **agreement, real conflict, different explanatory levels, and unknowns**. Preserve differences between historical artistic philosophy, practitioner experience, standards and empirical user evidence.
4. Choose a **conditional resolution**: "Use A under X; use B under Y; when uncertain do Z first." Avoid ranking famous designers by prestige.
5. State **counterexample and falsification**. What changed product fact, user evidence, or tested visual observation would reverse the choice?
6. Propose a **cheap observation/experiment** (task test, rendered visual study, prototype or logic check). A contrast/accessibility issue cannot be resolved by an artist's personal taste.
7. Record what actually happened. Only promote the synthesis to a core card if repeatable and real-task relevant.

## Synthesis S01 — Why vs composition vs user meaning (studied, transfer untested)
| Source and read scope | Original emphasis | Conditional transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Frank Chimero, *The Shape of Design*, Ch 1–2 (author web edition) | Purpose (why) and craft (how), distance/iteration, beauty as design concern | Before aesthetic changes, articulate what a visitor must understand/feel |
| Arthur Wesley Dow, *Composition*, 1914 ninth ed., opening & early harmony sections (no figures reviewed) | Line, notan, color; subordination/repetition/spacing/harmony | Use massing and relative dominance to support an already justified visual objective |
| Abby Covert, *How to Make Sense of Any Mess*, selected original web chapters | Information relations and language govern comprehension | Check whether names/categories accurately encode user mental model |

**Agreements (interpretation):** solving design problems entails relationships and purpose rather than mimicking a finished look. **Tension:** strong artistic subordination can mislead when multiple tasks are genuinely equal; simplification can hide a required domain choice; purposeful beauty is not quantified usability. **Resolution hypothesis:** first establish accurate user vocabulary/verb, then choose a compositional hierarchy that makes that decision visible without compromising secondary tasks. **Falsify with:** first-exposure user confusion or wrong choice, hidden essential actions, failed keyboard/zoom/contrast. **Results:** NOT RUN.

## Synthesis S02 — Reusability vs irreducible role semantics
- Frost, *Atomic Design*, Ch 2 (directly studied): component reuse and page feedback.
- Covert, selected IA sections: language, information relationships and meaning.
- Project interpretation: two composers can share form controls and state handling but should not automatically combine permission/recipient contracts. A shared *component* is not a shared *business operation*.
- Counterexample: genuinely identical business operations under separate routes may warrant consolidation.
- Outcome: hypothesis pending actual code and interaction evidence.

## Synthesis S03 — Historical attention vs contemporary task evidence
- Dow and Vignelli articulate purposeful hierarchy; William James offers historical habit/attention theory; 2025 HCI text selected Ch3 frames detection, recognition and visual search.
- Do not use James's 1890s physiology as empirical proof of modern UX behavior. Treat modern HCI findings only at the exact studied scope.
- Resolve “dramatic hero vs findable CTA” by checking real first-view content, task stakes, attention and actual keyboard/mobile experience. Neither simple aesthetic hierarchy nor a generic conversion button is automatically right.

## Syntheses are not results
None of these examples establishes that the v0.5 skill is better. Carry original reading and source status; record failures as first-class feedback. If after real use these do not change good decisions, remove them from the activation route and retain only scholarly notes.
