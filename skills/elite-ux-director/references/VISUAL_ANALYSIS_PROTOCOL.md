# Visual-Source Study — Observe Before Interpreting (v0.5)

**When:** the design argument depends on a real artwork, print spread, poster, chart, diagram, photograph, building, or visual example. **Otherwise don't load it.**

## Evidence record
`asset_id | creator | work/title | exact edition or collection | figure/page | provenance URL/file | visual inspection date | representation (original scan / derivative / thumbnail) | license/access | legibility coverage`.

Classify each statement as:
- **OBSERVED:** "On this original page, one large dark rectangle lies left of three smaller text groups." Only after image was actually inspected.
- **SOURCE SAYS:** what an author/caption argues, not automatically what the pixels show.
- **INTERPRETED:** our inference about narrative, movement, intent, mood, or gaze. Eye-tracking requires eye-tracking evidence.
- **TRANSFER HYPOTHESIS:** how a mechanism might operate in mobile/web UX; must be checked in that new medium.
- **NOT VISIBLE / UNKNOWN:** image unavailable, illegible or cropping means the claim can't be supported.

## Inspection, in order
1. Inspect **entire artifact** first: medium, boundaries, original aspect ratio, contextual text. Avoid analyzing only a popular crop.
2. Record **elements and relationships**: dominant visual masses; foreground/background; alignment/grid; proportion (described, not numerologically mythologized); contrast/value; type hierarchy, sizes and reading order; photographic subject/crop; rhythm/repetition; negative space; color in context; text/image relationship.
3. Record **function**: what information/action must the original communicate? Do not mistake a poster for an app form. Separate institutional/cultural/historical context from visual admiration.
4. Inspect critical subregions/figures at legible size. If the figure is blocked, text extraction alone does not qualify.
5. Analyze **three competing plausible meanings** when ambiguous rather than pretending designer intent is certain. Contextualize relevant critique/source arguments.
6. Propose a minimal **visual mechanism**, NOT a lookalike or asset clone: e.g., one dominant mass and two subordinate pathways; editorial leading; asymmetric contrast with predictable navigation.
7. Model **digital transfer constraints**: screen width, responsive reflow, content variability, language, keyboard, touch, reduced motion, low power, assistive tech and trust.
8. Reject/qualify transfers that harm real action clarity, legibility, disability access, context or brand authenticity.

## Comparative exercise
For a real authorized public homepage, preserve the message, destination and proof data. Make three original **massing thumbnails** that differ by reading order, hierarchy or layout mechanism (not random palettes). Inspect the rendered output at mobile and desktop. Assess comprehension and visual craft **separately**. A beautiful composition without a findable intended action is not product UX success.

## Visual-source caveats
Historical texts may have biased cultural framing; do not present the author's aesthetics as empirical universal truth. No golden-ratio or Gestalt law claims without matching evidence. Modern print adaptations must not invent exact pixel prescriptions from historical page sizes.

## Tiny image record template
```text
Source/edition/figure and URL:
Image directly viewed? resolution/extent:
OBSERVED composition (geometry/type/color/value/space):
AUTHOR claims (chapter/figure):
INTERPRETATION and plausible alternatives:
MECHANISM (not style label):
Digital transfer hypothesis:
Failure/counterexample:
Validation method and results or NOT RUN:
Rights/credit/reproduction boundaries:
```
