# Homepage Experience Intelligence — Eight Conditional UX Cards (v0.4)

**When to load:** a public homepage, product landing page or major audience-facing entry screen needs evaluation, redesign, concept selection or readiness review. **Do not load** for ordinary authenticated workflow repair or typography-only CSS tasks. Reuse the core's mode/work type and acceptance list. If a creative-direction skill already supplied a thesis, consume it; otherwise the core's Concept Exploration can supply one. Frontend/engineering skills own implementation mechanics. A homepage is not inherently a checkout or an ad landing page.

**Use:** select *up to two* H-cards that apply to the current user problem; run `python scripts/retrieve.py homepage H01` from the skill root when Python exists, otherwise read just the Markdown section. Combine with at most one relevant C-card/P-pattern as needed. Never load every card for an ordinary request.

**Evidence scope:** These are conditional syntheses of **public original articles and standards inspected 2026-11-15**, not entire books, Faneqo user observations, a controlled experiment, or rules proven for every site. Citation ledger and transfer cautions appear at the bottom.

## H01 — Five-second comprehension and accurate value
**Trigger:** hero is visually striking but a first-time visitor cannot say what product is, for whom, or why it matters. **Ask:** without brand recognition, can visitors identify product category, beneficiary, distinctive utility, and next action from actual rendered mobile/desktop hero? **Do:** derive a simple proposition from verified current product abilities; show a tangible artifact, short demonstration or believable example alongside evocative creative work. Test first-exposure comprehension through actual uncoached people when available; until then it is only a cognitive-walkthrough *prediction*. **Avoid:** making a huge text wall or a generic feature list. **Counterexample:** established cultural brands may prioritize emotion with an obvious adjacent product cue rather than a standard SaaS slogan.
**Source:** NN/g (Wang, 2024), *Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles*, principle 2; observational practitioner UX guidance, transfer not tested on this product.

## H02 — Segment by intent, not invented demographics
**Trigger:** homepage targets several actors (buyers, creators, fans, managers, existing customers) who need different next steps. **Ask:** what is the primary acquisition audience, what secondary visitors need navigation, and what page routes already exist? **Do:** map task-specific entry journeys and prioritize one primary path with clear secondary access; keep multiple nonidentical actor goals legible. **Avoid:** generic 3-persona cards, fake interview quotes, or assuming every visitor wants sign-up. **Counterexample:** deliberately one-purpose campaign landing pages may rightly minimize extra paths.
**Source:** NN/g Wang (2024) principle 4; Costanza-Chock *Design Justice* selected chapter (section-scoped study) for participation/evidence caution. Segment-specific effectiveness is a hypothesis.

## H03 — Show the real product and proof, without inventing trust
**Trigger:** beautiful photos/animated UI but visitors cannot tell what they'd get or whether claims are true. **Ask:** what user-visible workflow can we demonstrate *truthfully*, with current code/data? **Do:** show a realistic before→after user outcome, credible screenshots or runnable product preview, clearly distinguish illustrative/demo from verified public records, and inspect proof assets/licensing. **Avoid:** fabricated follower counts, unsupported conversion rates, fake brand endorsements or invented testimonials; a mocked screen must not masquerade as a live customer record. **Counterexample:** conceptual products without approved customer assets may need clearly labeled illustrative examples.
**Source:** NN/g Wang (2024) principle 3 and NN/g Nielsen (2002) real-content guidance; evidence of a particular claim must be verified from current product.

## H04 — Narrative sequence without losing navigation or agency
**Trigger:** cinematic landing page relies on scroll effects, complex timelines, tall chapter stacks, or page-wide animation. **Ask:** can a visitor grasp the offer and reach the action without watching the story? **Do:** model orientation → value → real example → how it works → trust/cost → action, reordering as necessary for audience. Ensure skip/jump, purposeful sections, truthful anchor links and a clear recovery path. Test whether the page falsely looks finished at the first fold and whether long-scroll landmarks work. **Avoid:** scrolljacking, forcing a chapter, cloning an admired studio's pacing, or treating scroll depth as conversion. **Counterexample:** an intentionally immersive creative portfolio may prioritize exploration, but still needs operable navigation and reduced motion.
**Source:** NN/g Wang (2024) principles 3–5 and W3C WAI carousel guidance; art narrative is creative interpretation, not empirically universal.

## H05 — Make the primary action explicit and verify the destination
**Trigger:** visitors cannot tell what a CTA does or multiple CTAs compete. **Ask:** what commitment does the button entail (explore demo, create account, begin trial, pay, contact)? **Do:** map each CTA to the real route and landing state; label the *outcome*, make its cost/commitment clear, preserve secondary routes for existing users; test auth, mobile menu, keyboard and no-JS/reduced-motion where appropriate. **Avoid:** vague “Get Started” that hides payment/trial conditions, nonfunctional demo buttons, deceptive urgency, or merging unrelated personas. **Counterexample:** a familiar low-stakes CTA can remain short when adjacent context makes outcome obvious.
**Source:** NN/g Wang (2024) principle 4; product destination/terms require verification on the actual application.

## H06 — Treat visual story and performance as a coupled constraint
**Trigger:** hero media, animated typography, video, photography, third-party embeds or immersive motion are central. **Ask:** how does it behave when image/video fails or on a low-end device, reduced-motion preference, zoom, slow connection, keyboard and screen reader? **Do:** keep real text and primary CTA readable without animation or media; reserve image space, optimize LCP resource priority and image choices, avoid hydration/navigation defects, reduce main-thread work. Document *measured* Core Web Vitals (mobile and desktop 75th percentile if field data exists) and lab results separately. **Avoid:** claiming Lighthouse alone proves accessibility or that performance budgets are met without measurements; decorative video cannot gate product comprehension. **Counterexample:** a deliberately immersive experience can use heavy media if essential actions still function and measured tradeoffs are accepted.
**Sources:** Google web.dev *Web Vitals* (current LCP/INP/CLS definitions), *Most Effective Ways to Improve Core Web Vitals*; W3C WCAG 2.2 Understanding 2.2.2 and 2.3.3. Practical synthesis, no page measured.

## H07 — Independent evidence before declaring a masterpiece
**Trigger:** team/founder or model rates the design “premium” or “clear” using just screenshots, source code or its own copy. **Ask:** were primary tasks, first-glance interpretation, real CTA navigation, responsive composition, focus and reduced-motion actually observed? **Do:** separate creative critique (distinctiveness/cultural authenticity/craft), UX comprehension (can unfamiliar visitor explain it/choose the correct next action), engineering (real links, performance and accessibility), and user outcomes (signup quality/retention if instrumented). Use a reviewer who did not implement it when feasible; label synthetic cognitive walkthroughs as hypotheses. **Avoid:** fabricated usability statistics, conflating conversion with user benefit or treating aesthetic scores as task completion. **Counterexample:** if no real people can be recruited, provide transparent risk review and real browser validation, not made-up interviews.
**Source:** NN/g Nielsen/Wang homepage guidance; Anthropic evaluation research (general method), and project evidence policy. This evaluation schema is our own.

## H08 — Preserve approved design identity and iterate where evidence warrants
**Trigger:** redesign touches an existing, sophisticated, partially locked homepage or component design system. **Ask:** what is approved, currently deployed, genuinely broken, and authorized to change? **Do:** record *invariants* for established composition, tokens, product language and user journeys; evaluate current visuals with real content and viewport-specific crop/readability before new directions. Reuse supplied creative direction; otherwise use this skill's Concept Exploration within unlocked decisions. Hand fixed content/behavior contracts to frontend implementers. **Avoid:** replacing a music-native identity with a generic marketing template, inventing fonts/tokens, or expanding scope into unrelated app modules. **Counterexample:** strong evidence of accessibility/functional failure can justify targeted changes to a previously locked area.
**Sources:** Brad Frost *Atomic Design*, Chapter 2 (section-scoped read); Vignelli *Canon*, selected Semantics/Syntactics; Lab `docs/MASTER_PRODUCT_PLAN.md` and official frontend-design comparison. This is process guidance.

## Source and transfer ledger — public articles/standards, not books
- **NN/g, Huei-Hsin Wang, Mar 15 2024**, *Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles*, original article read for five categories and examples. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/ . This supports *general diagnostic questions*, not any specific Faneqo conversion effect.
- **NN/g, Jakob Nielsen, May 11 2002**, *Top 10 Guidelines for Homepage Usability*, historical article reviewed. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/top-ten-guidelines-for-homepage-usability/ . Older examples and search-box size specifics do not automatically apply to a modern product homepage.
- **Baymard Institute, Edward Scott, Oct 13 2026 update**, *Homepage and Category Navigation UX 2026*, inspected original article https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-navigation-best-practice . **E-commerce-specific** data (180+ retail sites); transferable questions on navigation and mobile category discoverability, but *not* Faneqo startup/artist platform user data or conversion benchmarks.
- **Google Chrome/web.dev, Web Vitals**, original current documentation https://web.dev/articles/vitals . Good field targets: LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile, mobile/desktop segmented. These are standards/goals, NOT measurements of any reviewed page.
- **Google Chrome/web.dev, most effective CWV optimizations**, original technical guide https://web.dev/articles/top-cwv . Resource discoverability/prioritization, image dimensions and long tasks need investigation in each implementation; no optimization result claimed.
- **W3C WAI, Carousels Tutorial**, original guidance https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/carousels/ . Content changes, keyboard/focus, ability to stop/pause motion and access to hidden content; no suggestion every homepage needs a carousel.
- **W3C WCAG 2.2 Understanding 2.2.2 and 2.3.3**, current original technical explanatory guidance https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide and https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions . 2.3.3 is **AAA**, not required to claim AA; however offering reduced-motion alternatives is a sound design precaution. No conformance claimed.
- **Earlier book research:** Frost Ch2, Vignelli selected spreads, Keith/Lynch/Boulton scoped excerpts (see `SOURCE_LEDGER.md`). Read-status boundaries preserved.
