# Visual craft and lightweight design memory

## Before polish

Understand the subject, audience, task and real content. For major work, explore two or three distinct premises in rough compositions: change the organizing idea, focal structure, imagery/type relationship or sequence, not merely colors. Preserve approved choices and functional semantics. A practical constraint can produce an original concept; inspiration is not restricted to art books.

Use a few relevant actual reference screens when a visual question is unresolved. Open the images, state source/access limits, and extract a mechanism with a counterexample; do not imitate a recognizable identity. Relevant research and the Exceptional Interface Atlas are optional inputs, not prerequisites for every change.

## Review the actual render on separate axes

- **Idea and originality:** could this be transplanted unchanged into an unrelated product? What content-specific relationship makes it memorable? Is expressive ambiguity appropriate here?
- **Composition:** where does the eye go first, second and third? Do scale, alignment and grouping reflect meaning? Are primary actions legible without overpowering content?
- **Typography:** intentional roles, readable utility text, controlled wraps, line length/leading, no awkward clipping or arbitrary display treatments.
- **Space and rhythm:** meaningful grouping, cadence and density; remove redundant explanation/chrome without erasing affordances. Equal importance may justify equal weight.
- **Imagery and color:** authentic assets/provenance, subject-safe crops at each width, readable image/text relationships, purposeful contrast and restrained accents consistent with the product.
- **Interaction and resilience:** motion explains a change; focus, errors and reduced motion preserve meaning; originality does not hide the task.

Name the strongest successful relationship, then the few highest-leverage corrections with concrete locations and reasons. Avoid a single beauty score or claiming extraordinary quality from a checklist. Distinguish self-review, independent critique and owner judgment. Escalate unresolved concept choice or costly taste disputes, not every spacing adjustment.

## Learn without another system

Reuse the project's existing decision log, brief or visual workspace. Append a compact record only when a real verdict or useful failure occurs:

`date/version | concept + screenshot/state | decision maker | accepted/rejected/provisional | exact reason | observed/tested vs inferred | scope/conditions | next verification`.

Owner-approved is a distinct fact; a generated direction remains unapproved. Preserve rejected alternatives and any later reversal. Consult the most relevant prior verdicts before significant new design work; do not universalize one founder preference across products. Keep private screen evidence in the existing ignored workspace.

In a Lab checkout, reuse scripts/visual_workspace.py for image/finding/direction records and scripts/prepare_design_packet.py for a bounded optional research packet. Record verdicts in the existing project decision artifact; do not change the workspace schema merely to use this skill. A standalone installation does not require the Lab tools.
