# Personas and Live-Context Research Protocol — General-Purpose

**Research status:** Method, not validated product persona data. Any product-specific research belongs in a separate adapter. Do not overwrite or duplicate its source collection. Consult a source-backed dataset only for its actual product and current purpose.

## Persona evidence ladder
1. **Observed participant:** actual opt-in interview, field observation, behavioral session with method, date, audience/sample caveat.
2. **Published credible field research:** named source, population, method, limits, transfer conditions.
3. **Founder/stakeholder report:** valid report of experience, not a population metric.
4. **Code/product specification:** authoritative for current technical behavior, **not** for whether humans understand it.
5. **Provisional behavioral archetype:** model built from known constraints, marked a hypothesis to validate.
6. **Generated/illustrative persona:** teaching or scenario fixture only; never present as real person or validated research.

A picture, invented age/name, backstory, quote, or demographic label does not increase evidence quality. Avoid stereotyping by genre, age, disability, cultural background or stage of career.

## Persona/job card
```
Actor / role: [verified, reported or provisional]
Situation: [where, when, task pressure, environment]
Job to accomplish: [natural language, successful result]
Responsibilities and permissions: [verified backend/spec vs unknown]
Motivation and stakes: [evidence source]
Device and context: [lighting, movement, time, connectivity, accessibility]
Current behavior / tools / workarounds: [observed vs conjectured]
Interrupted scenario: [what breaks; resume expectation]
Critical harm to prevent: [privacy, wrong recipient, destructive action]
Alternative entry / novice-to-expert difference: [as applicable]
Design hypothesis, not requirement:
Contradictory / missing evidence:
What could falsify it:
```

## Multi-actor journey
When a journey crosses roles (artist → venue staff → fan → manager), map each actor's control and data visibility separately. Include consent, delegated authority, account switching, ownership, deletion/retention and post-event expectations. One actor's convenient workflow may create harm or confusion for another; articulate both perspectives.

## Situational UX
For real-world/event use, ask about: distraction, low light, noise, movement, crowd, one-handed interaction, battery/network, camera permissions, display glare, mobility/assistive tech and whether users want to participate at that moment. A fan can choose not to scan during the show and still deserve a usable later path.

## Evidence translation discipline
Present `observed condition → design consequence → source → alternative/counterexample → verification`. Do not turn a single interview into a statistical claim, or promote a mock persona to a real one. If no field study exists, provide a **small set of falsifiable scenarios** and implement only safe, reversible choices under the product contract.
